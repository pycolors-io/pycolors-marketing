## Overview

Starter Free gives you a **credible SaaS surface out of the box**.

Not a blank repo.
Not a backend-heavy template.
Not a toy demo.

It gives you a production-shaped UI with real screens, real states, and real navigation so you can validate your product direction before investing in backend complexity.

The goal is simple:

> run it in minutes, understand it quickly, and adapt your first product surface fast.

<Callout title="Why this matters">
  Most SaaS projects lose time before users ever see the product. Starter Free
  helps you make the product visible earlier, so you can validate UX, messaging,
  and flows before wiring the full business layer.
</Callout>

## Your first session

Start with the [installation guide](/docs/starter/installation), then open
`http://localhost:3000/dashboard` in your running starter. To explore without
installing, [open the Starter Free demo](https://starter-demo.pycolors.io/dashboard).
Paths such as `/projects` below belong to the starter, not the Marketing site.

The dashboard guide presents four suggestions, not a completion checklist.
Following a link does not complete a step or save onboarding progress.

1. **Try the project workflow.** Open `/projects`, choose **New project**, enter
   a sample name, and select **Create**. Open that project's details from its
   row, return to the list, and rename it using the row actions. Reload to check
   browser-local persistence before deleting your disposable example.
2. **Explore the assistant interface.** Open `/assistant` and choose a sample
   prompt. The [assistant guide](/docs/starter/assistant) explains the local chat,
   editable definitions and task simulation. No AI model is connected; leaving
   the page or reloading clears this session.
3. **Make the app yours.** In your own checkout, edit `app/layout.tsx` for the
   app name and metadata, `app/(app)/layout.tsx` for app-header branding, and
   `app/globals.css` for styles and semantic tokens. The
   [project structure guide](/docs/starter/project-structure) explains the files
   and the existing provider boundary; do not introduce a second project store.
4. **Evaluate connected foundations.** [Explore Starter Pro](/starters/pro) and
   [see what is included](/docs/starter-pro/what-is-included) when you need
   Auth.js authentication, protected routes, Prisma/PostgreSQL, and Stripe
   billing foundations. Pro is source code to configure and adapt. Buying it
   does not unlock the running Free demo or automatically migrate local projects;
   your project data model and workflows remain yours to implement. Use the
   [upgrade guide](/docs/starter/upgrade) to plan that work.

### Local data and reset

Project changes use this browser's `localStorage` when it is available. There
is no account, shared database, authorization, or synchronization between
browsers or devices. Use sample data, not sensitive information. The hosted
demo and a localhost checkout have separate browser storage.

**Reset demo** asks for confirmation, then replaces your local projects with
the original samples. It cannot be undone and does not reset the theme.
Clearing the starter's browser storage also removes saved projects. If saving
is unavailable, the visible **Local storage notice** explains that changes are
kept for the current visit only and will be lost after reloading. An unreadable
saved record falls back to samples with a notice rather than proving your old
projects were recovered.

Dashboard metrics, trends, activity, and gated previews remain fictional.
Keep the **Illustrative demo data** notice when adapting those values; they
are not live billing or analytics, and they do not count your local projects.
Authentication, billing, admin, and settings screens are still demo surfaces,
not connected account or payment services.

### Appearance

Use the sun/moon control to switch light and dark mode. First visits follow
the system preference; a saved explicit choice takes precedence on reload.
The preference is browser/origin-local, not an account setting. With unavailable
storage, switching works on the current page but may not survive a reload.

### Mental model

> Starter Free is a UX contract, not a full backend implementation.

You keep:

- screens
- navigation
- components
- states
- product structure

You replace later:

- mock data
- auth provider
- billing logic
- API calls
- database contracts

<Callout type="info" title="Frontend-first by design">
  Projects support browser-local creation, details, renaming, deletion, and
  reset. Authentication, billing, admin, settings, and dashboard figures remain
  demo surfaces. You still connect your own backend for shared, protected
  product data.
</Callout>

## Steps

<Steps>

<Step>
### Understand what is included

Starter Free gives you the main surface area of a SaaS product.

| Surface   | What it gives you                                                                       |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Auth UX   | Login, register, forgot password, loading and error-ready states                        |
| Dashboard | Three-step getting-started guide, navigation, and clearly labelled illustrative metrics |
| Projects  | Browser-local create, details, rename, delete, persistence, and confirmed reset         |
| Admin     | Demo members, roles, and invitation surfaces; no real invitation service                |
| Billing   | Illustrative plan, invoices, portal entry, and payment-method surfaces; no real payment |
| Settings  | Profile, organization, security, and danger zone structure                              |

This is enough to validate a real product direction before wiring production services.

</Step>

<Step>
### Know what is intentionally mocked

Starter Free does not connect these demo workflows to production business services. Browser-local project persistence is not a backend or an access-control boundary.

It does not include:

- database schema
- API routes
- Stripe integration
- session management
- email provider
- role-based access control

That is intentional.

```txt title="starter-free-boundary.txt"
Starter Free
  UI surface
  browser-local project workflow
  illustrative business data
  light/dark appearance

Starter Pro
  auth wiring
  billing wiring
  Prisma/PostgreSQL foundation
  your own product workflows to implement
```

The goal is to avoid backend friction before you know the product surface is worth wiring.

</Step>

<Step>
### Explore the vertical slice

Follow [your first session](#your-first-session) before inspecting the remaining
`/admin`, `/billing`, and `/settings` demo surfaces. Open project details from an
existing row; `/projects/[id]` describes the route pattern, not a literal URL to
paste. Keep the loading, empty, unavailable-storage, and missing-project states
when adapting the workflow.

</Step>

<Step>
### Adapt one product surface

Start small. Rename one core entity and adjust the first product flow.

Good first changes:

- rename “Projects” to your domain entity
- adapt illustrative dashboard KPIs without removing their demo notice
- change empty state copy
- adjust navigation labels
- add one new route
- update brand tokens

This is enough to create a credible demo or first MVP surface.

</Step>

</Steps>

### What you can ship quickly

After installation, you can produce a credible first version by changing:

| Task                              | Outcome                                                  |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rename “Projects”                 | Makes the starter match your domain                      |
| Adapt illustrative dashboard KPIs | Keeps the demo story specific without claiming live data |
| Rewrite empty states              | Makes the UX feel intentional                            |
| Adjust navigation labels          | Makes the product easier to demo                         |
| Change brand tokens               | Makes the interface feel owned                           |
| Add one route                     | Proves the structure can grow                            |

That is enough to:

- show a client
- pitch an MVP
- validate UX
- record a product demo
- test your product narrative

## Decision guide

Use Starter Free if:

- you are validating UX
- you are pitching a product
- you are building a frontend-first MVP
- you want product structure before backend
- you need something credible fast

Evaluate Starter Pro when you need Auth.js authentication, protected routes,
Prisma/PostgreSQL, and Stripe billing foundations. Review
[what is included](/docs/starter-pro/what-is-included) rather than assuming every
demo screen becomes a connected feature. You still configure providers and
implement your product-specific data model, authorization, and workflows.

### First run path

Use the [four-step first-session path](#your-first-session): try a disposable
project, explore the local assistant, personalize your checkout, then evaluate
connected foundations. The
first useful result is a project you can create, open, rename, and reload in
this browser, not a fictional metric or a checked onboarding counter.

<Callout title="Upgrade when wiring becomes the blocker">
  Starter Free validates the product surface. Starter Pro wires the business
  layer with production-shaped auth, billing, backend, and delivery foundations.
  → `/docs/starter-pro`
</Callout>

## Common questions

<Accordions type="single">
  <Accordion title="Is Starter Free a full SaaS backend?">
    No. Projects work locally in the browser, while the business surfaces use illustrative data. Shared accounts, protected data, and payments require your own backend integration or separately configured Starter Pro foundations.
  </Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I use it for a real MVP?">
  Yes, especially for demos, validation, frontend-first MVPs, and product
  exploration. For real users, real payments, and protected data, move to
  Starter Pro or wire your own backend.
</Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Should I delete the mocked features immediately?">
    No. First explore the flows. The mocked surfaces teach the intended product structure and give you a stronger starting point for your own implementation.
  </Accordion>
</Accordions>
