## Overview

Starter Free helps you **validate the product surface**.

Starter Pro provides **configurable authentication, billing, and database foundations**.

Free already lets you create a project, view its details, rename it, and delete
it. Projects persist in this browser's `localStorage` when storage is available.
Use **Reset demo** to restore the sample projects after confirmation. This is a
local project workflow, not a connected multi-user application: there is no real
account, shared database, authorization, or cross-device synchronization.

Use sample data, not sensitive information. Clearing browser storage removes
your changes. If storage is unavailable, changes last for the current visit
only and the app displays a notice. Project status, member counts, update labels,
and dashboard metrics remain illustrative. Try the
[Starter Free getting-started guide](/docs/starter/getting-started) before deciding
whether you need server-backed foundations.

Upgrade when the blocker is no longer the UI, but the production wiring behind it:

- real authentication
- protected routes
- Stripe Checkout
- billing portal
- webhook-driven billing state
- backend-ready product structure

<Callout type="info" title="Separate source, not a demo unlock">
  Starter Pro is separate source code to download, configure, and adapt. Buying
  Pro does not unlock the running Free demo or automatically migrate your app or
  local projects. It does not provision your services or deploy your app. You
  still implement your own domain data model, permissions, and workflows.
</Callout>

### The upgrade moment

Starter Free is the right choice when your question is:

> What should the product feel like?

Starter Pro becomes relevant when your question becomes:

> How do I build on existing auth, billing, and database foundations?

The decision is about integration depth, not whether Free can save a local
project. Compare the [included scope](/docs/starter-pro/what-is-included) with
what your product needs; configuration and validation still belong to you.

### What you keep

You can reuse your product direction and selected UI work, but there is **no
automatic in-place upgrade**. Keep your Free source under version control before
starting and work with the Pro source in a separate directory or repository.

Review what is useful to bring across:

- routes and pages
- components and layouts
- design-system customizations
- product copy and interaction decisions

Port one small UI slice at a time. Compare the actual imports, layouts, public
APIs, and versions in your downloaded release rather than overwriting Pro's
configured authentication or billing foundations with demo code.

Replace or implement deliberately:

- mock auth → configured real authentication
- mock billing → configured and tested Stripe integration
- mock access → server-side checks and product-specific permissions
- browser-local project data → your own domain model and server-backed workflows

<Callout type="info" title="Preserve your Free work">
  Your original Free source remains your reference. Local demo records are not
  imported into Pro automatically. Any data transfer needs an explicit mapping,
  validation, and authorization design that you implement; no migration command
  or automatic transfer is provided by this guide.
</Callout>

## Upgrade flow

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Preserve Free and inspect the Pro source

    Save your Free work under version control. After purchase, use the claim
    email to download and unzip Starter Pro into a separate directory. Read its
    README and compare the delivered versions before adapting your UI. Follow
    [delivery](/docs/starter-pro/delivery) and
    [Getting started](/docs/starter-pro/getting-started) for access and setup.
  </Step>

  <Step>
    ### Configure and validate locally

    Follow the downloaded release instructions for Node.js, pnpm, dependencies,
    a local PostgreSQL database, and environment variables. Configure the
    providers you intend to exercise using the
    [environment guide](/docs/starter-pro/environment-variables). Keep secrets
    private and use local development or test services, not production data.
    Confirm the baseline runs before porting a product-specific UI slice.
  </Step>

  <Step>
    ### Add authentication

    Use Pro's included auth foundation instead of copying Free's mock behavior.
    Configure credentials, email, and the OAuth providers you need, then verify
    sign-in and session behavior with the [auth guide](/docs/starter-pro/auth).
    A rendered login screen is not proof that authentication works.
  </Step>

  <Step>
    ### Protect product routes

    Bring across one reviewed page or component at a time. Keep Pro's existing
    guards and add server-side permissions appropriate to your domain. Validate
    both allowed and denied access; hiding a link in the UI is not authorization.
  </Step>

  <Step>
    ### Connect billing

    Configure the included Stripe Checkout, portal, and webhook foundations.
    Follow [billing testing](/docs/starter-pro/billing-testing) in test mode and
    check resulting billing state, not just whether the billing page renders.
    The purchase of Starter Pro does not configure your own Stripe account.
  </Step>

  <Step>
    ### Replace mock data

    Define your own project or domain model, persistence, validation, permissions,
    and workflows. Pro's Prisma/PostgreSQL foundations do not automatically turn
    Free's local projects into shared records. Treat any data transfer as a
    separate, explicitly designed implementation; do not point demo code at a
    production database or assume browser data is trusted.
  </Step>
</Steps>

After each ported slice, run the release's applicable checks and keep the last
working version available for rollback. Record unconfigured integrations as
unverified. Complete the
[production checklist](/docs/starter-pro/production-checklist) before deployment;
a successful local run is not production approval.

### Why not build it yourself?

You can keep Free and implement the integrations yourself.

Compare that work with adopting and configuring Pro's included foundations:

- authentication
- Stripe billing
- webhook handling
- route protection
- billing-state synchronization
- database and production setup

Neither choice removes your responsibility for product logic, validation,
deployment, or operation. Choose the path that fits your actual requirements;
this guide does not establish a measured time or revenue saving.

<Callout title="An integration-depth decision">
  Pro is useful when its included foundations match work you would otherwise
  implement. Review what ships and what still needs configuration before buying.
</Callout>

## Common concerns

<Accordions type="single">
  <Accordion title="Will I lose my Starter Free work?">
    Keep your original Free source under version control and set up Pro
    separately. Reuse selected UI deliberately after checking compatibility;
    buying Pro does not modify or automatically migrate your Free app or data.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I upgrade later?">
    Yes. Free already supports browser-local project workflows. Pro is a
    separate source-code foundation to configure when your product needs
    connected accounts, payments, and server-backed data.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need to migrate everything at once?">
    No. Validate the configured Pro baseline first, then port one UI slice at a
    time. Implement your own domain persistence and data-transfer plan; local
    projects are not transferred automatically.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why not stay on Starter Free?">
    Stay on Free for local validation, or keep it and build your own
    integrations. Choose Pro when its configurable auth, billing, and database
    foundations fit your requirements. Either path still needs product
    implementation and checks before launch.
  </Accordion>
</Accordions>

### Ready to move faster?

If you are still exploring, stay on Starter Free. If connected foundations are
your next need, review the Pro scope and setup responsibilities before buying.
